How to Build a Study Routine That Actually Sticks

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Article
Time Management
  • Amelia Hart
  • 12 August 2026
  • 6 min read
  • 8 comments

Most students do not fail because they lack ability. They fail because their study plan assumes a perfect week that never arrives. The routines that survive contact with real life are short, repeatable and forgiving.

Start from the timetable you actually have

Before adding anything, write down the fixed blocks: school, travel, meals, sleep and one evening off. Whatever remains is your real study budget — usually far less than students expect, which is exactly why planning matters.

A routine you keep four days out of five will always beat a routine you abandon after a week.

Use short, named sessions

“Study maths” is not a task. “Twenty minutes of quadratic word problems” is. Naming the topic removes the decision cost at the start of every session, which is where most study time quietly disappears.

A sample weekday block

  • 10 minutes reviewing yesterday's notes from memory
  • 25 minutes of new practice on one named topic
  • 5 minutes marking honestly and writing down one mistake

Review weekly, not nightly

Sunday is for looking back: which topics got skipped, which mistakes repeated, and what needs a tutor's help this week. Ten minutes of review prevents a month of drifting.

Make it visible

A paper wall chart or the planner in the student dashboard both work. What matters is that the plan lives somewhere other than in the student's head.
